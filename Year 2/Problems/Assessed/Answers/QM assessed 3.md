A particle of mass $m$ moves in one-dimension with the potential equal to zero. The particle is in a state described by the following wavefunction, which is a superposition of two energy eigenstates and is normalised to give unit probability over a length $a$. $$\Psi(x,t)=\sqrt[  ]{ \frac{2}{3a} }\left[ \cos\left( \frac{\pi x}{a} \right)e^{ -iE_{1}t/\hbar }+\sqrt[  ]{ 2 }\cos\left( \frac{3\pi x}{a} \right)e^{ -E_{3}t/\hbar } \right] $$
(a) Express the values $E_{1}$ and $E_{3}$ in terms of $a$, $m$ and $\hbar$. If the energy of the particle is measured, what possible values can be obtained and with what probabilities? [5]

$$\hat{E}\Psi=i\hbar \frac{ \partial \Psi }{ \partial t } =\sqrt[  ]{ \frac{2}{3a} }\left[ E_{1}\cos\left( \frac{\pi x}{3a} \right)e^{ -iE_{1}t/\hbar }+E_{3}\sqrt[  ]{ 2 }\cos\left( \frac{3\pi x}{a} \right)e^{ -iE_{3}t/\hbar } \right] $$
