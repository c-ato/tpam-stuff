The charged pion $\pi^{-}$ is a meson and primarily decays into a final state of a muon, $\mu^{-}$, and an anti-muon neutrino, $\bar{\nu}_{\mu}$. 

If the $\pi^{-}$ decays at rest, what is the momentum of the $\mu^{-}$ and the $\bar{\nu}_{\mu}$? Assume one-dimension along the $z$-axis with the $\mu^{-}$ in the positive $z$-direction and the $\bar{\nu}_{\mu}$ in the negative $z$-direction. 
$$m_{\pi}=139.57 \frac{MeV}{c^{2}}\qquad m_{\mu}= 105.66 \frac{MeV}{c^{2}}\qquad m_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}\approx 0\qquad c=1$$
$$p_{\mu}+p_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}=p_{\pi}=0\qquad E=m_{\pi}=139.57MeV=E_{\mu}+E_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}=\sqrt[  ]{ m_{\mu}^{2}+p_{\mu}^{2} }+\sqrt[  ]{m_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}^{2}+ p_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}^{2} }$$
$$=\sqrt[  ]{ m ^{2}_{\mu}+p_{\mu}^{2} }+\sqrt[  ]{ (-p_{\mu})^{2} }=\sqrt[  ]{ m ^{2}_{\mu}+p_{\mu}^{2} }+p_{\mu}\implies (m_{\pi}-p_{\mu})^{2}=m_{\mu}^{2}+p_{\mu}^{2}$$
$$\implies p_{\mu}=\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}{2m_{\pi}}=29.79\dots MeV \therefore p_{\mu}=29.79 \frac{MeV}{c}\qquad p_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}=-29.79 \frac{MeV}{c}$$
$$E_{\mu}=\sqrt[  ]{ m_{\mu}^{2}+p^{2}_{\mu} }=109.77\dots MeV\qquad P_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{109.77}{c} MeV\\
0 \\
0 \\
29.79 \frac{MeV}{c}
\end{pmatrix}$$
$$E_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}=\sqrt[  ]{ m_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}^{2}+p^{2}_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}} }=29.79 MeV\qquad P_{\bar{\nu}_{\mu}}=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{29.79\dots}{c} MeV \\
0 \\
0 \\
-29.79 \frac{MeV}{c}
\end{pmatrix}$$
What are the energy-momentum vectors of the $\mu^{-}$ and $\bar{\nu}_{\mu}$ in a frame of reference where the $\pi^{-}$ was travelling along the positive $z$-direction with a velocity of $0.6c$ ? [5 points]
$$\gamma(v_{\pi})=\frac{1}{\sqrt[  ]{ 1-0.6^{2} }}=\frac{5}{4}\qquad p_{\pi}=m_{\pi}v_{\pi}\gamma(v_{\pi})=104.67\dots \frac{MeV}{c}(A)$$
$$E'=\gamma(v_{\pi})(E-v_{\pi}p)\qquad p'=\gamma(v_{\pi})\left( p-v_{\pi}E \right)$$
$$P'_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}

-45 \frac{MeV}{c}
