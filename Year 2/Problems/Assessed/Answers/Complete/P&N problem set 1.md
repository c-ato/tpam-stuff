The $J/\psi$ is a meson consisting of a bound charm-quark anti-charm-quark pair, discovered in 1974 at both SLAC and Brookhaven National Laboratory in the USA. The radius of the $J/\psi$ is $\approx 0.2fm$. The spin of the $J/\psi$ is $1$, hence the $J/\psi$ is a boson, albeit not a fundamental boson. 

Estimate the minimum velocity of the charm-quark in the $J/\psi$ meson. Is the charm-quark velocity within the $J/\psi$ relativistic? 
What would be the range of a force produced by the exchange of a $J/\psi$ meson?

$$\Delta x\Delta p\geq \frac{\hbar}{2}\implies \Delta p=mv=\left( \frac{1.27GeV}{c^{2}} \right)v\geq \frac{\hbar}{2(2\times0.2\times 10^{-15})}=1.31\dots \times 10^{-19}Ns $$
$$\implies v\geq 5.83\dots \times 10^{7}=0.194\dots c\therefore v_{min}=0.194\dots $$
This is relativistic as $v \not\ll c$.

$$\Delta t\Delta E=mc^{2}\Delta t\geq \frac{\hbar}{2}\implies \Delta t_{max} = \frac{\hbar}{2(2\times1.27GeV)}=1.29\dots \times 10^{-25}s$$
The maximum distance, and range, is then given by the maximum speed, $c$.
$$x=c\Delta t_{max} = 0.038\dots fm$$
