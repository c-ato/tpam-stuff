1. Provide the geometric interpretation of the partial derivative of a function $z=f(x,y)$ at $y$ at the point $(a,b)$, i.e. $f_{y}(a,b)$
   The means the gradient of $z$ in only the $y$ direction at the point $(a,b)$ 
2. Show that $f_{xy}=f_{yx}$ in the following case
   - (a) $f(x,y)=x^{m}y^{n}$, where $m$ and $n$ are constants
     $$f_{x}=mx^{m-1}y^{n}\qquad f_{xy}=nmx^{m-1}y^{n-1}\qquad f_{y}=nx^{m}y^{n-1}\qquad f_{yx}=nmx^{m-1}y^{n-1}\therefore f_{xy}=f_{yx}$$
   - (b) $f(x,y)=e^{ xy }\cos x$,
     $$f_{x}=e^{ xy }y\cos x-e^{ xy }\sin x)\qquad f_{y}=xe^{ xy }\cos x\qquad f_{xy}=xye^{ xy }\cos x+e^{ xy }\cos x-xe^{ xy }\sin x$$
     $$f_{yx}=xye^{ xy }\cos x+e^{ xy }\cos x-xe^{ xy }\sin x\therefore f_{xy}=f_{yx}$$
