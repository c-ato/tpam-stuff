Membrane curvature due to type of liposomes, changes to membrane structure.

Curvature is defined by radius of curvature in 2 perpendicular planes called the principal curvatures.

Total curvature is the sum, and gaussian curvature which is the product
$$J=c_{1}+c_{2}\qquad K=c_{1}c_{2}$$
examples: cylinder, has $J$ but no $K$, saddle may not have $J$ but have a $K$, sphere has both

Gaussian curvature is generally conserved.

$\kappa$ is pertaining to the ability of a membranes flexibility, due to what lipids are found and phase - highly dependent on bilayer thickness.

$J_{S}$ is spontaneous curvature - avg curvature where no energy of both parts of a bilayer.

Different type of lipid types have different spontaneous curvature.

Motor proteins are used to move membr ane to change its curvature for dynamic, or changing composition of the membrane, but this is slow and inefficient as new lipids need to be transcribed produced and translerated. Another is the use of proteins that become wedged in with some target curvature, these can be added or removed as needed (regulated by translation, antagonists, blockers ect)

Scaffold proteins, is an example as can bind to the membrane forcing it into a shape, but can easily be regulared by Rac proteins which would stop it from bindings.

These can also be used to target specific areas of membrane as these proteins can attached to specific curvature by their shapes.
