proteins have primary, secondary, tertiarty and quaterary structures - sequence of molecules, local folding/structures, folding and overall 3d combined structures - involcing complexes sometimes.

20 different amino acid groups - 3 additional found in rare cases.

3 classifications of proteins, - non-polar, polar and electrically charged (zwitterions)

Thyll groups tend to bond with each other - locks quaterary structures.

AA are either non-essential or essential (essential cannot be produced in body). 9 are essential for humans.

Polymers often form due to condensatiion reactions, for AA this makes a peptide bond (ester but linking atom is N instead of O in esters)

Only certain angles are permited and these can be ploted in a Ramachalan diagram. - secondary structures

Hydrophobic causes these ends to fold inwards or into membranes, and hydrophilic the opposite. Can be locked/stabalised in place if disulfide bonds occur, or H bonds are present- teriarty structures

Ribbon views show tertiary structures, solvent surface view shows acidic, and basic regions.

Chaperones often help proteins fold correctly in the first place becuase there can be several local favourable folded state.

Denaturation breaks bonds breaking down tertiary protein structure.

Post-translational modification - adding extra structures after being made - and multimeric proteins. 

PTM example of acylation where a fatty acids attach and allow it to be transported as fatty acid part will embed into a membrane. Addition of cofactors like haem in haemoglobin. 

Most common is phosphorylation where phosphate $PO_{4}^{3-}$ is added to an AA in a protein, this can modify the protein shape or allow other proteins to bind to it. 

An enzyme that catalyses the phosphorylation of another protein is called a kinases. The opposite enzyme that removed phosphate groups is a phosphaase.
